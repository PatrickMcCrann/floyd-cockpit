# Idea Funnel

Ten ideas went in. One came out. This is the full funnel, ranked, with the reasoning that promoted or killed each one. Every idea was scored on value to CFOs, demo strength, buildability in the time allowed, whitespace against live vendors, and fit with Floyd's community-data strategy.

## The build

**Growth Decision Cockpit.** A planning tool for Copperline, a fictional Series B SaaS company at $12M ARR whose board wants 49% growth next year. Adjustable levers (new logos, churn, pricing, AE hires and timing, an acquisition toggle) drive a deterministic model: revenue bridge to target, cash and runway, covenant headroom, and when cash goes negative. An AI layer writes the decision memo and runs a Plan Integrity Check that flags contradictory plans, like a Q4 bookings target that requires four AEs productive in October while the hiring plan starts them in December. Chosen because it answers the growth question posed in my founder interview, matches the number one pain named in my CFO interviews (cash forecasting, still done in Excel, no FP&A team), and occupies the narrative-plus-judgment layer no vendor owns. The company is deliberately a SaaS business, not a membership organization: the tool has to put the reviewer in the user's seat, and The F Suite itself has no CFO.

## The roadmap (not built, deliberately)

**Benchmarking Answer Engine.** Conversational, percentile-aware answers on what companies at each stage use and pay: banks, insurance, payroll, tools, comp. Every existing option is a static PDF or a stale forum thread. A CFO I interviewed described this product unprompted, including the need for anonymity and currency. Not built because its real moat is F Suite member data, which a take-home cannot access. Positioned instead as where Floyd goes next: the prototype shows what AI does for one CFO; this shows what it does for a network of them.

## Vetted clean, held in reserve

**Board Meeting Prep Copilot.** Variance narrative plus anticipated board questions with grounded answers. Board tools (Diligent, I'mBoard) only do assembly and distribution. Largely absorbed into the cockpit's memo output.

**Diligence Readiness Checker.** Sell-side "are we ready?" gap analysis for a raise or M&A process. AI data rooms serve the buyer side only. Strongest fallback build.

**Fund CFO LP-Reporting Assistant.** LP letters and valuation commentary are still manual; entrants (Maybern, Vessel) are early. A contrarian play for a real but smaller F Suite segment.

**Membership-organization edition.** The same cockpit re-skinned for a membership business: tiers, renewals, event and sponsor revenue, program capacity. This was the original framing (a fictional community business modeled on F Suite economics) before deciding the demo should live in the reviewers' world. It survives as proof the model generalizes, and as the version a community operator would run. A related prior build applies the same planning approach to civic and municipal program budgets, which shows the pattern works anywhere a small team needs CFO-level influence without a CFO.

## Surfaced in interviews, reserved

**Expense Intelligence / GL coding.** Remove GL selection from employees; a five-word business purpose plus merchant data does the coding, with vendor normalization. A fractional CFO built this by hand and took coding accuracy from under 50% to 95%. Real pain and real savings, but Ramp and Brex ship AI coding, Ramp sits on The F Suite's own recommended tools list, and it is the textbook case of the caution a service-firm CEO gave in my research: anything easy to build in AI is easy to copy.

## Retired on duplication

**Standalone scenario chat.** Runway, Causal, Pigment, and Abacum all ship it. Survives only as one live scenario question inside the cockpit demo.

**Investor update generator.** Visible, Paperstreet, UpdateMate, and Cabal own the founder-tools lane.

**Month-end flux explainer.** Rillet and Numeric ship AI flux analysis natively inside the close platforms.

**AR collections prioritizer.** Growfin, Upflow, Tesorio, and Stuut, all AI-first.

**13-week cash / treasury monitor.** Nilus, Trovata, Panax, and Vesto make the category more contested than it first appears. The cash-goes-negative view survives as a cockpit feature rather than a product.

**Headcount plan sanity-checker.** TeamOhana owns the category. The benchmark-ratio angle folds into the benchmarking roadmap.
