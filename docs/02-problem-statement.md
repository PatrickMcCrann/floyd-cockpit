# Problem Statement

## The problem

High-growth companies make invest-or-conserve decisions faster than their finance function can model them. A founder decides to hire, sponsor, launch, or acquire in a conversation. The finance answer to "can we afford this, and what breaks if we're wrong?" takes days or weeks of spreadsheet work. So the CFO is structurally last to know: the decision happens, and finance picks up the pieces.

The result is that forecasting in these companies is damage control. It looks backward at what was just spent instead of forward at what the plan requires. One fractional CFO I interviewed put it plainly: founders spend like fish out of water, and finance is always catching up.

## Why it persists

Two structural facts keep finance teams pinned in catch-up mode.

First, the work is manual. Growth-stage companies rarely have an FP&A team. The CFO and a controller do it all, and the models still flow through Excel workbooks. Both CFOs I interviewed confirmed this; one had hand-built her own cash forecasting dashboard in Claude because no tool served her clients.

Second, finance is the only department whose deadlines do not move. Product releases slip. Deals push. But financials are due on covenant and regulatory dates, payroll clears on payroll day, and taxes file when taxes file. A team whose calendar is immovable has no slack to get ahead of the curve. The catch-up work crowds out the strategic work every single month.

## Who has this problem

The primary persona is the CFO (or deputy) of a high-growth company between roughly Series B and D, running a finance team of three to ten. The same pain, sharper, appears one level down-market: companies at $3M to $10M that are too small for a full-time CFO and rely on the owner plus a fractional. Both are core F Suite constituencies.

## What CFOs say they want

Two desires came through every conversation and survey. They want to answer the cash question instantly: when does cash go negative, how much do we need to draw, and what happens if one assumption moves. And they want benchmarking that is current and anonymous: what companies their size are using, paying, and doing, instead of stale forum posts and six-month-old surveys. The first desire is the problem this prototype attacks. The second is the roadmap, because it is the thing only a community's proprietary data can answer.

## The payoff if solved

Automate the catch-up work and the same small team moves from reactive to proactive: in the room before the decision instead of after it, pressure-testing the plan ("what must be true to grow from $4.5M to $6M without breaking cash?") rather than explaining last month's variance. That shift, from reporter to operating partner, is the whole value of the CFO seat at a growth company.

## What this prototype does not solve

It does not replace the GL, the close, or the accounting team. It does not attempt expense automation (Ramp and Brex own that lane, and Ramp is on The F Suite's own recommended tools list). And it deliberately keeps the AI away from the math: a deterministic model computes, the AI explains and recommends. Anything easy to build in AI is easy to copy; the defensible layer is judgment structure now, and community data next.
