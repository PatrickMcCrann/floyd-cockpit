# Process Timeline

How this project came together, start to submission. The short version: research first, people second, build last.

**Thursday, Aug 20.** Final interview with the founder. In that conversation he posed the real question: how does this business get from $4.5M to $6M on the programs running today? Received the take-home brief that evening: build an AI tool CFOs would genuinely use.

**Thursday evening.** Initial brainstorming in Claude. Parallel research pass in Perplexity on the resources, tools, and communities high-growth finance leaders already lean on, to see what lives outside the F Suite's walls.

**Friday, Aug 21 (morning).** Deep research sprint: The F Suite's member base and segments, CFO priority surveys (Gartner, Deloitte, NetSuite), and the community's own recommended AI tools list. Produced a ranked list of ten candidate problems, then vetted every one against the live vendor landscape so the build would not duplicate an existing product. Four ideas died in vetting. Three came through clean.

**Friday, Aug 21 (afternoon).** Primary research: interviews with two CFOs. One leads finance at a large-scale consumer brand, for the view from a mature, fully staffed finance organization. The other is a fractional CFO serving multiple growth-stage companies, for the view from the trenches where the tool would actually live. The fractional CFO walked me through the shadow accounting dashboards she had hand-built for her clients: cash forecasting with adjustable assumptions, budget versus actuals, credit card GL coding. Recorded a video walkthrough. Her number one answer, unprompted: cash forecasting is the biggest problem growth companies have, and it still lives in Excel.

**Friday, Aug 21, 5pm. Direction locked.** One build chosen, a documented reserve list behind it, and a one-page problem statement drafted. The interview question from Thursday became the spine of the prototype: a fictional high-growth company facing a grow-faster decision, and the tool a CFO would use to stress-test the plan before it's made. The company was deliberately modeled as a Series B SaaS business rather than a membership organization, so the tool speaks to the reviewers' own world instead of The F Suite's books.

**Weekend.** Build with an AI coding agent against pre-written acceptance criteria. Record the Loom. Package and submit.

The sequence matters: the tool was chosen after the market was mapped and real CFOs were interviewed, not before. Every idea that got cut is documented in the idea funnel, with the vendor that killed it named.
